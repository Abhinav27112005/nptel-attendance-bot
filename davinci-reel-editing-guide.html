0 What we're building

Understand the recipe before you touch a button.

The reel you linked is a vertical short-form video. Almost every reel in this style is made from the same 5 ingredients:

  1. Vertical frame — the video is tall, not wide. The format is 9:16 at 1080 × 1920 pixels. This is the #1 setting beginners forget.
  2. Several short clips — 5 to 20 small video pieces, each usually 0.5–2 seconds long.
  3. One music track — a trending song or beat that runs underneath everything.
  4. Cuts on the beat — every time the music "hits," the clip changes. This is what makes a reel feel professional.
  5. Polish — transitions, a touch of slow-motion, text, and color that makes it look vivid.
Mindset: Editing is just arranging small clips in a row and trimming the boring parts off each end. That's 90% of the job. Everything else is decoration.

1 Install DaVinci Resolve (100% free)

The free version is fully capable of everything in this guide.

  1. Open your browser and search "DaVinci Resolve download", or go to the Blackmagic Design website (blackmagicdesign.com/products/davinciresolve).
  2. Click the big Download button. You'll see two columns: DaVinci Resolve (free) and DaVinci Resolve Studio (paid). Choose the free one on the left.
  3. Pick your operating system — Windows, Mac, or Linux.
  4. It asks for your name and email (just to register the free download). Fill it in and download. The file is large (several GB) — this is normal.
  5. Run the installer, click Next through the steps, and restart your computer when it finishes.
Slow computer? DaVinci Resolve is heavy software. If it stutters, that's your hardware, not you. Close other apps, and in Playback ▸ Timeline Proxy Resolution ▸ Half to make preview smoother while editing.

2 The 7 "Pages" explained

Resolve is split into 7 rooms. You only need 3 of them for a reel.

Look at the very bottom-center of the screen after opening a project. You'll see 7 little icons. Each is a "page" — a different workspace:

Deliver ★Export the video
Only care about the ★ three: Edit (build the reel), Color (make it look good), and Deliver (save the final file). Ignore Fusion and Fairlight completely as a beginner.

3 Create a vertical 9:16 project

Do this FIRST, before adding any clips. Fixing it later is painful.

  1. When Resolve opens, you land on the Project Manager. Click New Project, give it a name like "My First Reel", and click Create.
  2. Look at the bottom-right corner for a small gear ⚙ icon (Project Settings). Click it.
  3. On the Master Settings tab, find Timeline Resolution. Open the dropdown and choose Custom.
  4. Type the width and height swapped to make it vertical: Width = 1080, Height = 1920.
  5. Set Timeline frame rate to 30 (good for social media). Set this now — frame rate locks once you add clips.
  6. Click Save. Your preview window should now be a tall rectangle. 🎉
Why it matters: If you skip this, you'll edit in a wide horizontal frame, and your reel will have ugly black bars on top and bottom when posted. Vertical-first saves you hours.

4 Import your videos & music

Getting your files into the app. This is the "add the videos and music" part.

Step A — Put your files in one folder first

Before opening Resolve, create a folder on your desktop (e.g. "Reel Project") and drop all your video clips and your music file (.mp3 / .wav) inside it. Keeping everything together prevents "media offline" errors later.

Step B — Import into Resolve

  1. Click the Edit page at the bottom.
  2. Top-left is the Media Pool — an empty box that will hold your files.
  3. The easiest way: open your folder in Windows Explorer / Finder, select all your clips + music, and simply drag them into the Media Pool box.
  4. Alternative: right-click inside the Media Pool ▸ Import Media, then select your files.
  5. If a pop-up asks "Change frame rate?", click Don't Change (you already set it in Step 3).
You'll now see thumbnails of every video and a waveform icon for your song, all sitting in the Media Pool, ready to use.

5 Build the timeline

The timeline is the strip at the bottom where you lay clips in order, left to right.

  1. In the Media Pool, right-click your music track ▸ Create New Timeline Using Selected Clips. This creates the timeline and lays your song down first. (Building to the music is the pro way to do reels.)
  2. Your song now appears as a green audio clip on an audio track (labelled A1) at the bottom.
  3. Now drag your video clips one by one from the Media Pool up onto the video track (labelled V1), placing them in the order you want, left to right.
  4. Press Spacebar to play and preview at any time. Press it again to stop.
Reading the timeline: Video lives on top tracks (V1, V2…), audio on bottom tracks (A1, A2…). The vertical white line is the playhead — it shows exactly which frame you're viewing.

6 Fit horizontal clips into the vertical frame

If your clips were filmed horizontally (sideways), they'll have black bars. Here's the fix.

  1. Click a clip on the timeline once to select it (it gets a red outline).
  2. Look at the top-right for the Inspector panel. Click it to open.
  3. Under Transform, find Zoom. Drag its value up (e.g. to 1.7–2.0) until the clip fills the whole tall frame.
  4. Use Position X / Y to slide the clip so the important subject is centered.
  5. Repeat for each horizontal clip. (Clips filmed vertically on a phone already fit perfectly — no action needed.)
Shortcut: Instead of typing numbers, click the clip and drag directly in the preview window to reposition and resize it visually.

7 Cut & trim your clips

Trimming = shaving off the boring start/end of each clip so only the good bit remains.

Trim the ends (the gentle way)

Hover your mouse over the left or right edge of a clip until the cursor becomes a bracket [ ], then click and drag inward. The clip gets shorter. Drag outward to bring footage back.

Cut a clip in half (the blade)

  1. Move the playhead to the exact spot you want to cut (click on the timeline ruler, or use arrow keys).
  2. Press Ctrl + B (Windows) / Cmd + B (Mac). The clip splits into two at the playhead.
  3. Click the piece you don't want and press Delete to remove it. This leaves a gap.
  4. To remove a piece and automatically close the gap, select it and press Shift + Delete (this is called a ripple delete).
Golden rule of reels: Keep clips SHORT. If a clip feels a tiny bit too long, it's too long. Fast pace is what makes reels addictive.

8 Sync cuts to the beat 🥁 (the real secret)

This single technique separates amateur edits from ones that feel "professional."

The idea: mark where the beats hit in your song, then make every clip change happen exactly on a beat.

Step A — Mark the beats

  1. Click the audio (music) track once to select it.
  2. Press Spacebar to play the song.
  3. Every time you hear a strong beat/drum hit, tap the M key. A little marker drops on the timeline at that moment.
  4. Let the whole song play while tapping M on the beat. You'll end up with a row of coloured markers = your "cut points."

Step B — Cut clips to the markers

  1. Turn on snapping by pressing N (so the playhead magnetically sticks to markers).
  2. Drag the edge of each video clip so it starts and ends on a marker. Now every clip is exactly one "beat" long.
  3. Play it back. Each clip should flip to the next right as the music hits. That's the magic. ✨
Even easier method: On the Cut page, tools like "Sync Bin" and the fast trim make beat-cutting quicker once you're comfortable. But the marker method above works perfectly and is easiest to understand first.

9 Add transitions

Transitions blend one clip into the next. Use them sparingly.

The instant way

Click the cut point between two clips (a thin line appears), then press Ctrl + T. A default Cross Dissolve (smooth fade) is added.

The picky way (choose a style)

  1. Top-left, click Effects (or the "Effects Library" toolbox icon).
  2. Open Video Transitions. You'll see options like Cross Dissolve, Dip to Color, Blur, Push, Zoom.
  3. Drag the transition you like directly onto the cut point between two clips.
  4. To make it longer/shorter, drag the edges of the little transition rectangle that appears.
Less is more: Most punchy reels use hard cuts (no transition) on the beat, plus the occasional zoom or whip-pan transition for flair. Don't put a fancy transition on every cut — it looks cheap.

10 Slow-motion & speed ramps

Slowing a clip down for dramatic moments, or speeding it up to skip ahead.

Simple speed change

  1. Right-click a clip ▸ Change Clip Speed.
  2. Set speed below 100% for slow-motion (e.g. 50%), or above 100% to speed up (e.g. 200%).
  3. Click Change.

Speed ramp (fast → slow in one clip)

  1. Select the clip and press Ctrl + R to open Retime Controls.
  2. A speed bar appears under the clip. Click the little arrow ▸ Add Speed Point where you want the speed to change.
  3. Drag each section's arrow to set different speeds — e.g. fast into a moment, then slow at the peak. This is that dramatic "whoosh then freeze" look.
Buttery slow-mo: For smoother slow motion, right-click the clip ▸ Retime Process ▸ Optical Flow. (Best results if the clip was filmed at a high frame rate like 60fps.)

11 Add text & titles

On-screen words, captions, or a hook at the start.

  1. Open Effects ▸ Titles.
  2. Drag Text+ (or plain "Text") onto a video track above your clips (V2), at the point where you want the words to appear.
  3. Select the text clip, open the Inspector (top-right), and type your words in the Text box.
  4. Change the Font, Size, Color, and drag it into position in the preview.
  5. Make it appear/disappear by trimming the text clip's length on the timeline.
Keep text readable: Big font, high contrast, and keep it on screen long enough to read twice. On phones, thin small text disappears.

12 Color grade — make it pop

Colour grading is what gives reels that vivid, cinematic look. Basics only here.

  1. Click the Color page at the bottom (the ★ page with the colour-wheel icon).
  2. At the top, click the thumbnail of the clip you want to grade.
  3. Find the Primary Wheels (four colour wheels). Under them are sliders:
    • Lift = shadows/darks
    • Gamma = midtones
    • Gain = highlights/brights
  4. Nudge Contrast up slightly, and Saturation up a little (e.g. to 55–65) for a punchier look. Don't overdo it.
  5. To copy your look to every clip: right-click a graded clip's thumbnail ▸ hover the mouse over another clip and press the middle mouse button, or select all clips and use Ripple/paste grade.
Instant look with a LUT: In the LUTs panel (bottom-left of Color page), double-click a preset (try the "Film Looks") to instantly apply a cinematic colour style to the selected clip.

13 Music & audio levels

Make sure the music sits at a good volume and starts/ends cleanly.

  1. Back on the Edit page, look at your green music clip. Running through it horizontally is a thin volume line.
  2. Drag that line up to make it louder or down to make it quieter.
  3. Fade in/out: hover over the top-left or top-right corner of the audio clip — a little white handle appears. Drag it inward to create a smooth fade so the song doesn't start/stop abruptly.
  4. Trim the song to match your video's length (drag its right edge in), and add a fade-out at the very end.
Watch the meters: On the right you'll see audio level meters. Keep the loud peaks around -6 dB and never let them hit the red at the top (0 dB) — that causes ugly distortion.

14 Export for Instagram / YouTube Shorts

Turning your project into a single video file you can upload.

  1. Click the Deliver page at the bottom (rocket icon).
  2. Top-left, choose a preset. Pick YouTube or Custom. (These already use the H.264 format phones love.)
  3. Set Filename and choose a Location (Browse to a folder you'll remember).
  4. Confirm Resolution = 1920 × 1080 shown as vertical (1080 × 1920), Format = MP4, Codec = H.264, Frame rate = 30 (match your project).
  5. Click Add to Render Queue at the bottom.
  6. On the right, your job appears in the queue. Click Render All.
  7. Wait for the progress bar. When done, your finished .mp4 is in the folder you chose — upload it straight to Instagram Reels or YouTube Shorts. 🚀
Quality vs size: If you want smaller files, lower the "Quality" bitrate a bit. For social media, the default is already great — don't overthink it.

16 Common beginner mistakes (avoid these)

  1. Forgetting vertical setup — always set 1080×1920 in Project Settings before editing (Step 3).
  2. Clips too long — reels live and die on pace. When in doubt, cut it shorter.
  3. Ignoring the beat — random cuts feel messy. Cutting on the beat (Step 8) fixes almost everything.
  4. Overusing transitions & effects — one or two well-placed ones beat a dozen flashy ones.
  5. Loud/distorted music — keep peaks under 0 dB and add a fade at the end (Step 13).
  6. Not saving — Resolve auto-saves, but hit Ctrl+S often anyway.
  7. Copyright music — for Instagram/YouTube, use the platform's own audio library or royalty-free tracks so your reel isn't muted or blocked.
Your first reel won't be perfect — that's fine. Make three short reels this week. By the third, all of this will feel automatic. Editing is a skill you build with reps, not talent you're born with.
